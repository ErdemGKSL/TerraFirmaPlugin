# Outside parity (NOT-PARITY)

Things this plugin will **not** make identical to TerraFirmaCraft. Some differ on purpose, because the project owner decided
so. Some cannot be built for vanilla clients on Paper/Folia with CraftEngine, BetterModel and BetterHud. Others are deferred
or out of product scope. The parity loop and the auditor read this file before they open or re-open work:

- **Workers** (`PROTOCOL.md`): if an item reaches something listed here, build the "what we do instead" column and stop
  there. Do not try to reach TFC's exact behaviour, and do not open a follow-up item for the difference.
- **Auditor** (`INVENTORY.md`): an entry marked `audit: skip` is not a gap. Do not add it to the inventory as missing and
  do not create an item for it. The items listed under an entry still exist for the part of the feature that *is* in parity.

TFC source: `/opt/tfc` at `c3b4d9536b82e5a9746dd793c586720a74108057` (the ledger pin). All `tfc_refs` paths below are
relative to that checkout and were checked to exist on 2026-10-22. `B/` is short for
`src/main/java/net/dries007/tfc/`.

Entry fields: **id** `NP-<AREA>-nn` (stable, never renumbered; a removed entry keeps its id struck through), **TFC**
(what TFC does, with `tfc_refs`), **ours** (what we do instead, or nothing), **why**, **items** (the related ids in
`plans/parity/items`), **audit** (`skip` on every entry). Items that are partly outside parity carry a
`see_not_parity` line under their heading pointing back here.

Sources used: `INVENTORY.md` (impossible-on-vanilla table), the blocked items (`PAR-CLIM-05`, `PAR-DATA-04`,
`PAR-REL-09`), the Divergences table of `docs/porting/ledger.md`, `docs/hud/catalogue.md`, `plans/hud-layered-redesign.md`,
the decisions in `plans/gaps.md`, the locked decisions in `ARCHITECTURE.md` and `CLAUDE.md`, and the non-goals in `PRD.md`.

## A. Deliberate design differences (owner decisions)

The plugin could match TFC here but does not, by decision. Revisit only when the project owner changes the decision.

### The HUD as a whole

TFC draws its HUD in `B/client/IngameOverlays.java` (and `B/client/BarSystem.java`) from the sprite sheet
`src/main/resources/assets/tfc/textures/gui/icons/overlay.png`. Our HUD is a redesign: BetterHud elements generated by
`tools/datagen` (`HudLayouts`, `HudArt`), laid out in `docs/hud/catalogue.md` §5 and `plans/hud-layered-redesign.md`. The
HUD must be readable, not a pixel copy of TFC. The values behind the bars stay at TFC parity: the health scale (850/1000 at
average nutrition), the thirst and hunger values and the thresholds. Each element that differs is listed below. The
`PAR-HUD-*` and `PAR-SURV-04` item files carry no `see_not_parity` line yet, because HUD and drinking work was in progress
on 2026-10-22. These entries apply to them anyway.

#### NP-HUD-01: HUD rendered by BetterHud, not TFC's overlay layers
- **TFC**: NeoForge GUI layers registered by `IngameOverlays` (`HEALTH`, `FOOD`, `THIRST`, `MOUNT_HEALTH`, `EXPERIENCE`,
  `JUMP_BAR`, `HUD_MOVER`, `INK`, `CHISEL`, `FAMILIARITY`). tfc_refs: `B/client/IngameOverlays.java`, `B/client/BarSystem.java`.
- **Ours**: BetterHud boss-bar and core-shader elements fed by our placeholders and listeners (`tfp_health`, `tfp_thirst`,
  `tfp_hunger`, `tfp_air`, `tfp_armor`, `tfp_date`, `tfp_ambient_temp`, `tfp_held_temp`). Vanilla hearts, food, air and
  armour rows are hidden with transparent sprites. BetterHud's demo compass is switched off by the installer.
- **Why**: Vanilla clients cannot load GUI layers. BetterHud is the locked HUD decision (`CLAUDE.md`, `ARCHITECTURE.md` §9).
- **Items**: PAR-HUD-01, PAR-HUD-04, PAR-HUD-05, PAR-HUD-06, PAR-HUD-07.
- **audit: skip**

#### NP-HUD-02: Two-layer bar art in our own colours instead of TFC's overlay sheet
- **TFC**: Bars are slices of `overlay.png`. The food bar is green. tfc_refs:
  `src/main/resources/assets/tfc/textures/gui/icons/overlay.png`, `B/client/IngameOverlays.java`.
- **Ours**: Every bar has two pixel-aligned layers: a background (frame and empty interior) and an interior-only fill that
  BetterHud clips by value. Both are derived from one generated master by `tools/images/hud_layers.py`. The style is plain:
  a 1 px pure-black frame and indexed-colour PNGs. The colours are red health, **orange** hunger, blue thirst, light-blue
  air and steel-grey armour. Health is 81x10 and the other bars are 81x7.
- **Why**: The owner asked for the redesign (2026-10-22). A green hunger bar sat next to the green XP bar (ledger divergence
  row "TFC's HUD bars come from its overlay.png sheet").
- **Items**: PAR-HUD-01, PAR-HUD-02, PAR-HUD-06.
- **audit: skip**

#### NP-HUD-03: Armour bar instead of the vanilla armour icons
- **TFC**: TFC keeps the vanilla armour icons above its health bar. tfc_refs: `B/client/IngameOverlays.java`.
- **Ours**: The vanilla armour row is hidden (`armor_*.png` made transparent). A thin 81x7 steel-grey bar (`tfp_armor`, on
  vanilla's 20-point scale) sits directly above the health bar, with one empty row between them. It shows only while
  `tfp_armor > 0`.
- **Why**: The owner asked for one bar language (2026-10-22). The vanilla icons touched the health frame.
- **Items**: PAR-HUD-05.
- **audit: skip**

#### NP-HUD-04: Compact air bar instead of the vanilla air bubbles
- **TFC**: TFC keeps the vanilla bubbles above the thirst bar. tfc_refs: `B/client/IngameOverlays.java`.
- **Ours**: The bubbles are hidden. A flat light-blue 81x7 air bar sits directly above thirst. It shows only while
  `tfp_air < 300`, and its fill turns red below 60 air (3 s).
- **Why**: The owner asked for one bar language for the right column. The visibility rule is the same as vanilla's.
- **Items**: PAR-HUD-01, PAR-HUD-06.
- **audit: skip**

#### NP-HUD-05: Black health-text shadow
- **TFC**: The health text has a dark-red shadow (`HEALTH_TEXT_SHADOW_COLOR` 104, 0, 0). tfc_refs: `B/client/IngameOverlays.java`.
- **Ours**: A black shadow (`outline: FF000000`).
- **Why**: The dark red looked muddy over the grey empty channel of the two-layer bar (screenshot review 2026-10-22).
- **Items**: PAR-HUD-06.
- **audit: skip**

#### NP-HUD-06: Date, season and ambient temperature on the HUD
- **TFC**: The date and the climate appear only in the inventory's calendar and climate screens. tfc_refs:
  `B/client/screen/CalendarScreen.java`, `B/client/screen/ClimateScreen.java`.
- **Ours**: A top-left stone plate (124x22) shows the date and season text, a thermometer bar (-20..40 °C) and the
  temperature text. The player tab screens also exist (NP-UI-02).
- **Why**: The plans put date and climate readouts on the HUD (M2-CAL-02, M2-CLIM-03).
- **Items**: PAR-HUD-06, PAR-SURV-07.
- **audit: skip**

#### NP-HUD-07: Held-item temperature as a HUD popup
- **TFC**: The temperature of a held item appears only in its tooltip. No HUD element shows it. tfc_refs:
  `B/common/component/heat/HeatCapability.java`.
- **Ours**: Besides the tooltip line, a BetterHud popup shows `[tfp_held_temp]°C` (whole degrees) while the held item is
  hot.
- **Why**: Our addition (`docs/hud/catalogue.md` `m3-surv-06-held-temp-popup`). It is an extra, not a parity target.
- **Items**: PAR-HUD-08.
- **audit: skip**

#### NP-HUD-08: HUD geometry follows vanilla columns, not TFC's HUD mover
- **TFC**: `HUD_MOVER` shifts the vanilla left and right heights for TFC's XP style, and the bars use TFC's own offsets.
  tfc_refs: `B/client/IngameOverlays.java`.
- **Ours**: The left column (armour, then health) uses vanilla's heart columns. The right column (air, thirst, hunger) uses
  vanilla's food columns, mirrored. Every bar ends above the XP row (H-31) with one empty row between bars
  (`HudLayoutsTest`).
- **Why**: BetterHud cannot move vanilla elements. The layout leaves the XP bar, its number and the hotbar untouched.
- **Items**: PAR-HUD-05, PAR-HUD-07.
- **audit: skip**

### Commands and feedback

#### NP-CMD-01: `/tfp debug ...` and other test commands
- **TFC**: There are no such commands. TFC's commands are `/tfc ...`. tfc_refs: `B/common/commands/TFCCommands.java`.
- **Ours**: TFC's commands are ported under `/tfp` (PAR-DATA-01). We also add debug and test commands that TFC does not
  have: `/tfp debug hunger|thirst|heat|warm|chunk|worldgen|packets|place ...`, `/tfp time`.
- **Why**: The headless test harness and the screenshot checks need exact states. These commands are tooling, not parity
  targets. Do not remove them, and do not count them as divergences.
- **Items**: PAR-DATA-01, PAR-HUD-03.
- **audit: skip**

#### NP-UI-01: The action bar carries transient look-at and hold information
- **TFC**: TFC draws short-lived information as crosshair overlays and HUD layers: glassblowing steps, hoe readout, crate
  contents, chisel mode, animal familiarity, Jade/TOP block info. tfc_refs: `B/client/GlassblowingOverlays.java`,
  `B/client/HoeOverlays.java`, `B/client/CrateOverlays.java`, `B/client/IngameOverlays.java`.
- **Ours**: One channel, the vanilla action bar, shows the text when the player uses the item or sneak-looks at the block.
  The propick result goes to the action bar as TFC sends it. No BetterHud element is made for each overlay.
- **Why**: Owner decision. It is cheap, readable on every GUI scale and needs no extra HUD art. The overlays themselves are
  impossible (NP-CLIENT-03).
- **Items**: PAR-PROC-16, PAR-FARM-01, PAR-DECO-12, PAR-TOOL-08, PAR-TOOL-09, PAR-FAUNA-05, PAR-DATA-12.
- **audit: skip**

### Visuals and blocks

#### NP-VIS-01: Visual budget profiles (ore grades may share a look)
- **TFC**: Each ore has 3 grades on each rock, every one with its own texture. tfc_refs:
  `src/main/resources/assets/tfc/textures/block/ore`.
- **Ours**: The visual budget profiles in `ARCHITECTURE.md` §9.1 decide this. The default on 1.21.x is `level-3a`: poor and
  normal share a visual and rich stays distinct. The default on 26.x is `level-2`: all grades are distinct, deposits do not
  depend on the rock, and the 9 gem ores look like rock until they are exposed. The owner can choose another profile.
- **Why**: Vanilla clients have a limited number of block states, and some must stay free for other CraftEngine packs.
- **Items**: PAR-DECO-15, PAR-WORLD-03, PAR-WORLD-09.
- **audit: skip**

#### NP-VIS-02: Metal blocks, mechanical blocks, crates and workbenches drawn as display entities
- **TFC**: These are block models. tfc_refs: `B/util/Metal.java`, `B/common/blocks/rotation/AxleBlock.java`,
  `B/common/blocks/CrateBlock.java`.
- **Ours**: A carrier block with CraftEngine `entity_renderer` display entities. Hardened rock shares raw rock's visual.
  Byte-identical model sets share one state.
- **Why**: The visual budget (approved in `ARCHITECTURE.md` §9.1). The look stays the same and only the mechanism differs.
- **Items**: PAR-DECO-09, PAR-DECO-12, PAR-MECH-01, PAR-MECH-02, PAR-WORLD-15.
- **audit: skip**

#### NP-VIS-03: Repurposed vanilla blocks are never created in TFC worlds
- **TFC**: Lava and water make cobblestone or obsidian. Copper oxidises, coral dies, grass and moss spread, and so on, as in
  vanilla. tfc_refs: `B/mixin/LavaMixin.java`, `B/mixin/WaterMixin.java`.
- **Ours**: A vanilla block whose client look is one of our blocks is never created (lava and water make `stone`; the rest
  is cancelled), by `core/visuals/Suppression` and `paper/suppression/SuppressionListener`.
- **Why**: A real instance of such a block would look like TFC content on the client (`ARCHITECTURE.md` §9.1).
- **Items**: PAR-DATA-10.
- **audit: skip**

### Simulation, data and platform

#### NP-SIM-01: Lazy calendar evaluation instead of ticking
- **TFC**: Block entities, players and animals tick. For example, exhaustion is added as a float every player tick, and
  thirst is stepped once per check. tfc_refs: `B/common/blockentities/TickableBlockEntity.java`,
  `B/common/player/PlayerInfo.java`, `B/util/calendar`.
- **Ours**: State is a stored timestamp plus base values, computed on read and caught up on chunk load, on interaction and
  in a visible-area sweep. World reactions are decided async and applied sync. The results equal TFC's stepping except for
  float rounding: a hunger point is exactly 3000 ticks, and thirst is rounded once per gap.
- **Why**: Locked threading decision (`CLAUDE.md`, `ARCHITECTURE.md` §5). Folia regions and the MSPT budget.
- **Items**: PAR-INFRA-02, PAR-INFRA-11, PAR-SURV-03, PAR-SURV-04, PAR-FAUNA-05.
- **audit: skip**

#### NP-WORLD-01: Deterministic orders and the chunk-boundary rule in worldgen
- **TFC**: Some sums and draws follow identity-hash iteration order, which changes between JVM runs: biome weights, noise
  samplers, the hot-spring magma rock. Features see the whole 3x3 world-gen region as earlier features left it. tfc_refs:
  `B/world/ChunkNoiseFiller.java`, `B/world/feature/HotSpringFeature.java`, `B/world/feature/plant`.
- **Ours**: Fixed orders (sorted by biome id, the config order). A chunk runs the features of itself and its 8 neighbours
  and writes only its own blocks. Golden values match within float rounding.
- **Why**: Reproducible worlds and async, order-independent generation. TFC's own order cannot be reproduced either.
- **Items**: PAR-WORLD-02, PAR-WORLD-11, PAR-WORLD-13, PAR-WORLD-14.
- **audit: skip**

#### NP-DATA-01: Strict data loading and TFC bugs not reproduced
- **TFC**: Codecs ignore unknown keys and parse JSON leniently. A few edge cases behave oddly:
  `FluidComponent.fill` reports a negative fill when the content is over capacity, the scraping cell `(int)(hit * 4)` wraps
  into the next row at hit = 1.0, and a NaN temperature counts as snow. tfc_refs: `B/util/data/DataManagers.java`,
  `B/common/component/fluid/FluidComponent.java`, `B/common/blockentities/ScrapingBlockEntity.java`,
  `B/util/tracker/WeatherHelpers.java`.
- **Ours**: Unknown keys, duplicate keys and non-strict JSON fail the whole load, with every error listed. Ingredient
  arrays accept any ingredient type (a superset). The fill is 0, the cell is clamped, and NaN gives NONE. All TFC data
  still parses unchanged.
- **Why**: To catch datagen mistakes at build time and to avoid fluid loss. None of these edge cases occurs in normal play.
- **Items**: PAR-INFRA-04, PAR-INFRA-06, PAR-PROC-05, PAR-CLIM-02.
- **audit: skip**

#### NP-PLAT-01: Platform and versions
- **TFC**: NeoForge on MC 1.21.1. tfc_refs: `gradle.properties`, `build.gradle.kts`.
- **Ours**: Paper and Folia only, on MC 1.21.11 and 26.3 (`content/compat.yml`), with Java 25. 1.21.1-1.21.3 are refused
  at startup. 1.21.4-1.21.10 are unsupported because BetterHud's Java 25 bytecode does not load on that remapper.
- **Why**: Locked decision (`CLAUDE.md`). The three client plugins need 1.21.11+.
- **Items**: PAR-REL-03.
- **audit: skip**

## B. Impossible on vanilla clients (closest equivalent)

These need a client mod. The column "ours" is the substitute a worker builds, and nothing beyond it.

#### NP-FLUID-01: Custom fluids in the world
- **TFC**: Salt, spring and river water, molten metals and other TFC fluids exist as world blocks. River water pushes
  entities and shows flow particles. The aquifer fills with TFC fresh water. tfc_refs: `B/common/fluids/TFCFluids.java`,
  `B/common/fluids/RiverWaterFluid.java`, `B/common/blocks/RiverWaterBlock.java`, `B/common/blocks/MoltenFluidBlock.java`,
  `B/common/blocks/HotWaterBlock.java`.
- **Ours**: Vanilla water and lava in the world. Salt water is decided by the biome, and the river current is server-side.
  Hot spring healing is a server rule on vanilla water. Non-vanilla fluids exist only inside containers: barrels, jugs,
  buckets, vessels, crucibles and channels. Bucket placement of a non-vanilla fluid is disabled.
- **Why**: Clients cannot receive new fluid types (PRD non-goal).
- **Items**: PAR-FLUID-01, PAR-FLUID-02, PAR-FLUID-03, PAR-FLUID-04, PAR-WORLD-07, PAR-WORLD-15.
- **audit: skip**

#### NP-CLIM-01: Continuous climate colours
- **TFC**: The client tints grass, foliage and water per block and per frame from the instant temperature and the
  groundwater (colormaps). The client also gets the chunk data by packet. tfc_refs: `B/client/TFCColors.java`,
  `B/client/ClimateRenderCache.java`, `B/client/ColorMapReloadListener.java`,
  `src/main/resources/assets/tfc/textures/colormap`, `B/network/ChunkWatchPacket.java`.
- **Ours**: A datapack ships seasonal biome variants (16 colour bands for each base biome). An async remap of biome ids in
  the chunk packets picks one variant per 4x4x4 cell for the current month. The colour changes in steps and is quantised.
  No chunk-data packet is sent.
- **Why**: The client tints by biome, not by position (locked decision). The band resolution is an open owner decision
  (blocked item PAR-CLIM-05), but continuous tint stays impossible whatever is decided.
- **Items**: PAR-CLIM-05, PAR-CLIM-06.
- **audit: skip**

#### NP-CLIM-02: Client-side snow and rain look by season
- **TFC**: A mixin replaces `Biome.getPrecipitationAt`, so the client shows snow at or below 0 °C from the climate model.
  tfc_refs: `B/mixin/BiomeMixin.java`, `B/util/tracker/WeatherHelpers.java`.
- **Ours**: The server decides snow or rain correctly and accumulates snow from that decision. The client's falling
  particles follow the variant biome's fixed `temperature`. A band edge at 0 °C is open in PAR-CLIM-05.
- **Why**: Client-side rendering, and a mixin is impossible.
- **Items**: PAR-CLIM-02, PAR-CLIM-05.
- **audit: skip**

#### NP-CLIM-03: Sky, stars and solar rendering
- **TFC**: A custom star map, and the sun position from latitude and season. tfc_refs: `B/client/overworld`,
  `B/client/overworld/SolarCalculator.java`, `src/main/resources/assets/tfc/stars.json`.
- **Ours**: Nothing. The vanilla sky stays. The server-side solar rules (day length, climate) are ported.
- **Why**: Client rendering (PRD non-goal).
- **Items**: none (ledger row only).
- **audit: skip**

#### NP-UI-02: Player inventory tabs
- **TFC**: Calendar, nutrition and climate tab buttons inside the player inventory screen. tfc_refs:
  `B/client/screen/CalendarScreen.java`, `B/client/screen/NutritionScreen.java`, `B/client/screen/ClimateScreen.java`,
  `B/network/SwitchInventoryTabPacket.java`.
- **Ours**: A click outside the inventory window, where the resource pack draws a button, opens our chest-style GUI.
- **Why**: The player inventory screen cannot be changed without a mod (locked decision).
- **Items**: PAR-SURV-07.
- **audit: skip**

#### NP-CLIENT-01: Key bindings
- **TFC**: Keys to place an item (V), cycle the chisel mode, stack food and pour faster. tfc_refs:
  `B/client/TFCKeyBindings.java`, `B/network/PlaceBlockSpecialPacket.java`, `B/network/CycleChiselModePacket.java`,
  `B/network/StackFoodPacket.java`, `B/network/PourFasterPacket.java`.
- **Ours**: Sneak + right-click to place items, sneak + swap-hand to cycle the chisel mode, automatic food stacking, and
  sneaking while pouring to pour faster.
- **Why**: Vanilla clients send no custom key events.
- **Items**: PAR-POT-01, PAR-TOOL-09, PAR-METAL-13, PAR-SURV-01.
- **audit: skip**

#### NP-CLIENT-02: Mob effect icons, item heat colour and custom model loaders
- **TFC**: Custom effect icons (thirst, exhausted, overburdened, ink, glow ink). Hot items are tinted and their tooltips
  refresh every frame. Armour icons use the `tfc:trim` model loader. tfc_refs: `B/common/effect/TFCEffects.java`,
  `src/main/resources/assets/tfc/textures/mob_effect`, `B/common/component/heat/HeatCapability.java`,
  `B/client/ClientEventHandler.java`, `B/client/model/TrimmedItemModel.java`.
- **Ours**: Effects ride vanilla effect carriers, and HUD or action-bar indicators show them. The heat glow and the tooltip
  are resolved when the item packet is sent, and refresh only on the next packet. Armour icons are plain `item/generated`.
  A `select` on `trim_material` may come with the trims.
- **Why**: The client registries, the item colour handlers and the NeoForge model loaders are client-side.
- **Items**: PAR-DATA-07, PAR-HEAT-01, PAR-HEAT-02, PAR-DATA-11, PAR-TOOL-03.
- **audit: skip**

#### NP-CLIENT-03: Crosshair and world overlays, ghost previews, the Jade/TOP readout
- **TFC**: Text under the crosshair (glassblowing, hoe, crate), ghost block previews, highlight boxes, and Jade or The One

  `B/client/CrateOverlays.java`, `B/client/IGhostBlockHandler.java`, `B/client/IHighlightHandler.java`, `B/compat/jade`,
  `B/compat/theoneprobe`.
- **Ours**: Action-bar text on use or on sneak-look (NP-UI-01). There are no ghost previews or highlight boxes.
- **Why**: Client rendering, client mods.
- **Items**: PAR-PROC-16, PAR-FARM-01, PAR-DECO-12, PAR-DATA-12.
- **audit: skip**

#### NP-CLIENT-04: Client config styles
- **TFC**: Each player picks a health display style, food expiry tooltip style, heat and climate temperature style, time
  delta style, disabled-XP-bar style and rotation display style. tfc_refs: `B/config/ClientConfig.java`,

  `B/config/ClimateTemperatureDisplayStyle.java`, `B/config/DisabledExperienceBarStyle.java`,
  `B/config/RotationDisplayStyle.java`.
- **Ours**: Server-wide config keys with the same names and TFC's defaults. There is no per-player choice.
- **Why**: There is no client config on vanilla clients.
- **Items**: PAR-HUD-07, PAR-INFRA-05, PAR-SURV-02, PAR-DATA-06.
- **audit: skip**

#### NP-CLIENT-05: Create TFC World screen
- **TFC**: A world-creation screen with TFC's generator settings. tfc_refs: `B/client/screen/CreateTFCWorldScreen.java`,
  `B/mixin/WorldPresetsMixin.java`.
- **Ours**: The `bukkit.yml` generator entry plus our config file.
- **Why**: A client screen. Server worlds are not created from the client.
- **Items**: PAR-WORLD-16.
- **audit: skip**

#### NP-PART-01: Ambient particles
- **TFC**: Butterflies, moths, wind, falling leaves, leaves and steam, shown without any player action. tfc_refs:
  `B/client/particle/Butterfly.java`, `B/client/particle/Moth.java`, `B/client/particle/WindParticle.java`,
  `B/client/particle/FallingLeafParticle.java`, `B/client/particle/TFCParticles.java`.
- **Ours**: Dropped. Particles appear only for explicit actions (pouring, hammering, lighting a fire), using vanilla
  equivalents.
- **Why**: There are no custom particle types on vanilla clients, and the stream of packets they would need is too costly.
  Locked policy (`CLAUDE.md`).
- **Items**: PAR-DATA-09, PAR-GATHER-04.
- **audit: skip**

#### NP-ENT-01: New entity types
- **TFC**: Each animal is its own entity type, with its own hitbox, pathing, synced data and renderer (it scales age every
  frame). tfc_refs: `B/common/entities/TFCEntities.java`, `B/common/entities/livestock/TFCAnimal.java`,
  `B/common/entities/livestock/CommonAnimalData.java`.
- **Ours**: A vanilla carrier mob for each TFC living type (`core/animal/carriers.tsv`, with the hitbox deltas recorded)
  plus a BetterModel model. Data lives in the carrier PDC `tfp:animal`. The age stage is derived lazily on a staggered
  20-tick task.
- **Why**: Vanilla clients cannot get new entity types or synced data.
- **Items**: PAR-FAUNA-01, PAR-FAUNA-02, PAR-FAUNA-03, PAR-FAUNA-04, PAR-FAUNA-21.
- **audit: skip**

#### NP-SURV-01: Drinking from open water with an empty hand
- **TFC**: An empty-hand right-click on any water sends `PlayerDrinkPacket`. tfc_refs: `B/network/PlayerDrinkPacket.java`,
  `B/common/player/PlayerInfo.java`.
- **Ours**: A drink works when the click hits a block behind the water, such as a lakebed in view (`WaterDrinkListener`).
  Open water with nothing behind it needs an item in hand. Which item is an owner decision (`plans/gaps.md`).
- **Why**: A vanilla client sends no packet for an empty-hand click that hits nothing (checked on the wire, run
  `spikes/run/wire3`).
- **Items**: PAR-SURV-04.
- **audit: skip**

## C. Deferred or out of product scope

Not built now. Each one is reopened only by the owner decision named below.

- **Why**: A PRD open question, and a licence and branding rule.

#### NP-GUIDE-01: Form of the field guide
- **TFC**: A Patchouli book (7 categories, 81 entries) with recipe pages. tfc_refs:

## D. How an entry moves between this file, the items and the ledger

1. **Into this file.** A worker or the auditor finds a TFC behaviour that we will not match. Then:
   - Record a ledger Divergences row first (`docs/porting/ledger.md`: TFC, ours, reason, revisit).
   - Add the NP entry here, with the next free number in its area, and `audit: skip`. Section A needs an owner decision,
     with its date or source. Section B needs the vanilla-client reason and the substitute. Section C needs the decision
     that would reopen it.
   - If an item covers the feature, add `see_not_parity: NP-...` under the item's heading. Keep the item for the part that
     is in parity. Do not change its status for this. A fully covered item with no remaining work is closed by the owner,
     not by the worker.
   - In `INVENTORY.md`, the feature row may cite the NP id. A feature that is only an NP entry gets no new item.
2. **Out of this file.** The owner reverses a decision, or a technique appears that makes the behaviour possible on vanilla
   clients (for example a new CraftEngine or BetterHud capability). Then:
   - Strike the entry through here (`~~NP-...~~` plus one line: why, date). Never delete or renumber it.
   - Open an item in `plans/parity/items/<AREA>/` (the next free id, its own `files_owned`, a wave after its deps), and
     add the row to `STATUS.md` and the README coverage matrix.
   - Remove the `see_not_parity` reference to this entry from the related items.
   - Update or close the ledger divergence row when the item is done.
3. **Between sections.** Move an entry from C to A or B when its owner decision is taken, for example when the field
   guide form is chosen and it still differs from Patchouli. The id stays the same.
4. **What does not belong here**: temporary gaps that an open item will close (those stay in the items and in
   `INVENTORY.md`), and exact ports that differ only in mechanism but give the same result (those need no entry).
